# Sponsor update composition rubric

Sponsor updates should feel proportional to what volunteers actually observed. The composer counts visits and words in distinct `Volunteer:` transcript observations; repeated observations, interviewer questions, companion-profile length, and photo count do not make an update richer.

| Source material                                              | Expected regular-update narrative                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| One visit, or fewer than 80 volunteer-observation words      | One or two short paragraphs; usually 40–100 words, never over 120, and shorter when facts are scarce                                           |
| Multiple visits with at least 80 volunteer-observation words | Usually 100–220 words, never over 250; each paragraph needs a distinct observed detail                                                         |
| Graduation/adoption story                                    | Usually 100–220 words and never over 280, using both the existing adoption seed and recent observations; shorter when those sources are sparse |

The pinned postscript is appended after generation and is not part of these narrative limits.

## Review checklist

- Facts are traceable to volunteer observations or the supplied graduation seed.
- The text uses warm, plain rescue language without headings for a sparse update.
- Subject, teaser, body, and captions complement one another instead of repeating a sentence.
- There are no generic gratitude paragraphs, repeated facts, flowery filler, invented emotional meaning, or stronger medical/behavioral claims than the source supports.
- Photo references and captions remain factual, and extra photos do not cause extra narrative.

## Sparse example

Source: one volunteer writes, “Biscuit chased her blue ball, then curled up for a nap.”

Avoid padding that observation into a long introduction, a generic paragraph about the value of sponsorship, and a sentimental conclusion. A suitable narrative is one short paragraph: “Biscuit made time for both play and rest during her visit. She chased her blue ball, then curled up for a nap.”
